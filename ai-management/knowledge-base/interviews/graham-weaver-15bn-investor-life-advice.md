# I Asked a $15BN Investor for Life Advice

> **Source:** YouTube — "I Asked a $15BN Investor for Life Advice" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zy7Nld2_JSw)
> **Speaker:** Graham Weaver (Founder & CEO, Alpine Investors)
> **Interviewer:** John (YouTube host)

## Key Takeaways

### 1. Success = Tiny Daily Habits, Not Big Moves

Graham's advice to his early-20s self: success comes down to **habits**, not sweeping moves or massive events.

> "If I look back, it's a sum of tiny tiny little habits — working out every day, sleeping really well, writing down my goals, writing down the things I'm going to do each day to work toward my goals, doing stuff when you don't want to do it."

The "boring answer" is the real answer. No divine inspiration, no crypto windfall — just the work you're supposed to be doing **today**.

### 2. The First Million: Buy a Small Business

Graham's playbook for going from zero to the first million:

1. **Find a small private business** (e.g., car wash) with the best location/potential
2. **Finance with debt**: seller note, mortgage on the building, bank loan on business value — minimize equity required
3. **Operate the hell out of it**: make every customer thrilled
4. **Learn, then buy another one, and another**

He did exactly this with label printing companies starting at age 25.

> "The mistake people make is they go do what everyone else is doing. Your fortune is probably not in crypto because everyone in the world's doing that. Go where other people aren't going."

### 3. It Takes Decades — Expect That

- Started buying label printing companies in **1997**
- Lost money on **5 of his first 8 deals**
- Lost money on his **first fund**
- It took **a year** just to raise the first fund
- Got flattened during the **Great Recession** — companies in default, drained savings to make payroll twice
- Took **5 years** to raise the next fund after the recession

> "Go in with the expectation that it's going to take like 10 years. And people don't want to hear that. But that's okay because your whole life is that ride — that's the fun of life, that process of going toward it."

### 4. The Wrestling Story: Never Quit Again

In high school, Graham was the #1 ranked wrestler in his district. After losing one match to the #2 ranked kid, he quit — and regretted it for the rest of high school.

In college, he took up rowing. Got cut freshman year. Seat-raced out of the boat sophomore year. But he wrote in his journal **every single day**: *"I am the best rower in the United States and I am never going to quit no matter what happens."*

By senior year: **Captain of a National Championship rowing team** with one of the top erg times in the world.

> This experience gave him the persistence framework he applied to Alpine — the expectation that it would take time, and the knowledge that if he stayed with it, it would eventually work out.

### 5. Flow: The Real Source of Happiness

Referencing Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi's book *Flow*:

> "What really makes you happy is being completely present in the moment in whatever you're doing. That could be cooking, writing a book, reading a book, doing a podcast — anything where you're just locked in."

Hitting goals can actually be **depressing** because the expectation doesn't match reality. The real joy is in the flow state during the journey.

### 6. Stress Comes from Conflict, Not Hard Work

> "Stress actually comes from conflict — when you have two values or two things that are in conflict. Hard work doesn't actually create stress. Stress is when you're working hard on something you're not excited about."

When Graham shifted from corporate to Alpine, he thought he was using 80% of his potential at the corporate job. After experiencing 100%, he revised that to **5%**.

### 7. Schedule Innovation

Alpine has ~20 things no other PE firm does. These come from **scheduled innovation**:

**The four questions they ask:**
1. What's not going well that we need to fix?
2. Where are bottlenecks breaking down?
3. What could kill us that we need to extinguish?
4. **What's going well that we could scale?** ← 90% of their returns came from this question

### 8. Energy Management

Graham's personal energy regimen:
- **Sleep** is the #1 priority — goes to bed early, gets up early, sleeps a lot
- **Strict diet** — optimized supplements and nutrition
- **No caffeine, no alcohol, no sleeping pills, no gluten**
- Claims to have **the same or more energy at 51 than at 21**

### 9. Play a Different Game

On competing with Blackstone, KKR, Apollo:

> "You have to play a different game. If you're going out and bidding on the same things that everyone else is bidding on, you're not playing a winnable game."

Alpine's edge: **Manufacturing their own management teams**. They hire people from Stanford, Harvard, and other business schools, build leaders, then deploy them as CEOs of companies that don't come with leadership (corporate carve-outs, founder exits). Very few firms fish in that pond.

### 10. The Talent Revelation

During the Great Recession, Graham's executive coach JP Flom forced a root-cause analysis of every fire Graham was trying to extinguish. Every time, the root cause was **the wrong person in a key role**.

> Coach: "Graham, you've told me you want to build the number one PE firm in the world. Are you going to do that with B-minus people in these key positions?"
>
> "At that moment I literally wrote down in my journal: I am in the talent business. I'm not in the private equity business. I'm in the talent business first and foremost."

The difference between an A and a B player over a decade: not 10%, but **200-300x**.

### 11. Hire for Attributes Over Experience

Conventional PE: hire a CEO for a healthcare software company → search for people who already run healthcare companies → narrow pool.

Alpine's approach: **Hire for attributes** — persistence, drive, will to win.

> "The number one thing is the will to win. Are you someone who, when you put your name on something, it happens? That will leap out of an interview."

### 12. The Three-Part Framework for Success

**1. Know where you are**
- Who are you at your very best?
- What are your limiting beliefs?
- Write them down — they stop being scary fears and become to-do items

**2. Know where you want to go**
- What would you do if you knew you wouldn't fail?
- Think in 5-10 year terms, not 1-2 years
- Suspend the "how" — *"The how is the killer of all great dreams"*

**3. Know how to get there**
- Make a list of 30 things you could do to move toward your goal
- List 6 people who could help
- List things you could read
- List people who have done it before

### 13. The Power of Writing Goals

> "When you write your goal every day, a part of you starts to assume the identity of someone who's already achieved that goal."

95-98% of thoughts are subconscious and tend toward the negative. Writing goals redirects that 95-98% toward where you want to go.

### 14. The Billboard Message

If Graham could put one thing on a billboard:

> **"Great things take time."**
